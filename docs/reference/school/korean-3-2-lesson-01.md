# Korean 3-2 · Lesson 1: Helping others

This is a Lesson 1 pilot, not a claim that the whole textbook course is authored. The textbook and workbook remain the main source for reading, listening, conversation, writing, and teaching the grammar.

Source: 재외동포를 위한 한국어 3-2, textbook pp. 14–23 and workbook pp. 4–8. Vocabulary comes from textbook p. 17. Permission examples come from textbook p. 18 and workbook p. 7. Intention followed by a request or suggestion comes from textbook p. 19 and workbook p. 7. Quiz sentences are new applications of those patterns, not quotations. The accompanying transcripts are evidence aids; consult the source PDF for visual context.

The 19 cards comprise 13 vocabulary entries and six model expressions. Dictionary verbs are cues, not complete sentences. The three -(으)ㄹ 테니까 cards are clause fragments, deliberately ending in “so…” in English; they need a following request or suggestion. Multiword vocabulary uses the app's phrase-card format. Romanization is a pronunciation aid; Hangul recognition is the learning goal. Synthesized Korean and English audio is a model to listen to, not a pronunciation grade. Cue images show the English meaning without revealing the Korean answer.

Introduce a few cards each weekday. Defaults remain four new cards, five per round, and a 15-minute sitting cap. The learner may practice more when he wants. Sorting cards into “know” does not unlock paper. Every required card must pass the ladder's two recognition directions and matching. Typing and speaking are useful extra practice; typing is not required for this Lesson 1 paper gate.

When ready, the learner chooses **Print quiz** from the course screen. Printing is explicit. A second press resumes or reprints the same outstanding attempt. Use the answer sheet identified on that printed quiz, and mark the corresponding A–D rows. Paper examples are bilingual, with English intent and Korean completions. All six learning targets must eventually be answered correctly; there is no 80% shortcut.

After scanning, correct question credits remain. Each unresolved grammar question has a brief explanation and a mapped expression card. **Review these cards** presents the meaning and model, then requires fresh recognition in both directions. A missed check keeps that card pending. Earlier practice, simply viewing the card, and ordinary spaced review do not satisfy this retry gate. Explain the grammar before another quiz if the learner recognizes the expression but still cannot apply it.

The next explicit print asks only the unresolved questions and switches form. A two-question retry does not make the learner redo four correct questions. Forms A and B use different examples while retaining the same learning-target identifiers. Further retries alternate the forms; there are two forms, not an unlimited bank of new examples. All six questions here assess grammar application, so a paper miss does not demote vocabulary mastery. Future direct-vocabulary questions may opt into the existing paper-miss demotion rule.

Ambiguous or blank scan marks follow the existing teacher review process. Do not invent a grade to unlock a retry. Teacher corrections update cumulative credits; a correction can reopen a question and require new focused checks. Ordinary spaced-review misses after completion do not reopen this lesson.

Daily card work and lesson completion are separate. The learner can meet today's card goal while the paper quiz remains pending. “Lesson complete” means all six grammar targets are resolved. His other courses and programs remain enrolled.
