# Korean 3-2 · complete sixteen-unit course

The course follows 재외동포를 위한 한국어 3-2 and its workbook: ten grammar-introduction units, five review units (3, 6, 9, 12, 15), and a cumulative final (16). The source book is designed for one unit per week; the app advances by demonstrated learning, with no forced weekly deadline. Introduce a few cards each weekday using four-new-card, five-per-round and fifteen-minute sitting defaults. Existing lessons and evidence are retained.

There are 284 unique bilingual cards, 32 pinned print forms, and 120 cumulative grammar targets. The primary vocabulary list of every source unit is represented, including the themed vocabulary introduced in review units. Repeated words and model expressions share card identity and learning history across decks. Review units use earlier grammar in fresh contexts; Unit 16 reuses the complete acquired card pool and introduces no new cards.

## How to use each lesson

Read the textbook dialogue and explain the two grammar patterns before expecting paper application. Work through the workbook exercises alongside the app. Vocabulary cards give dictionary forms; model-expression cards give short sentences or meaningful clauses. English cue images represent meaning without revealing Korean. Romanization is a reading aid; Hangul recognition and listening remain the goals.

Only the current lesson is a required daily card program. Future decks remain locked until preceding paper targets are resolved; completed decks are available for optional practice and their results remain in history. A day's card goal is independent of finishing the whole lesson. Old words still receive spaced review when later decks open.

Every required card must pass recognition in both directions and matching before **Print quiz** unlocks. Simply viewing a card, sorting it into “know,” elapsed time, typing, or saying the word does not establish this paper readiness. Printing is always explicit. An outstanding quiz is reprinted as the same attempt, not replaced with a new attempt.

All learning targets must eventually be correct. After scanning, previously correct targets remain credited. Misses identify model cards and give English grammar feedback. **Review these cards** shows the model and requires fresh correct recognition in both directions after the miss. Explain the grammatical distinction when the expression is recognized but the rule is still unclear. Then print a retry that asks only unresolved targets, using the alternate form. Forms A and B alternate; they are two authored examples per target, not unlimited new examples.

The final quiz has twenty targets, one for each grammar pattern taught in the book. It is a cumulative sampling assessment; it does not claim to test every verb, every vocabulary item, or every source exercise. Its focused reviews point to previously learned model cards. All quiz targets in this course assess application, so paper misses preserve vocabulary mastery. A later ordinary spaced-review miss does not revoke paper completion.

Teacher review handles uncertain scans, blanks, and ambiguous marks. Voided targets remain unresolved. Corrections can reopen a specific target without erasing unrelated later passes. Existing teacher attestations and curriculum exceptions continue to control progression.

## Grammar, register and teaching notes

### Unit 1: Helping others

- **-아/어도 돼요?** — Ask permission to do an action; use the stem's -아/어 form before 도 돼요. The polite question fits asking a parent or teacher. Source: textbook p. 18; workbook p. 7.
- **-(으)ㄹ 테니까** — State the speaker's intended action before a request or suggestion. These model cards are clauses: the listener needs the following action to complete the meaning. Use -을 after a consonant and -ㄹ after a vowel or ㄹ stem. Source: textbook p. 19; workbook p. 7.

### Unit 2: Reading and diary writing

- **-(ㄴ/는)다 / -았/었다** — Diary and written plain statements: present action verbs take -ㄴ다 after a vowel or ㄹ (drop ㄹ), -는다 after other consonants. Present adjectives and 있다/없다 keep -다. Past verbs and adjectives take -았/었다. Source: textbook p. 28.
- **-(으)ㄴ/는/(으)ㄹ 것 같다** — Express an inference, not certainty. Past actions use -(으)ㄴ, current actions and 있다/없다 use -는, current adjectives use -(으)ㄴ, and predictions use -(으)ㄹ 것 같다. Nouns use 인 것 같다. Source: textbook p. 29.

### Unit 3: Expressing feelings

- **-아/어도 되다** — Ask for or grant permission with -아/어도 되다. This differs from obligation (-아/어야 하다) and prohibition (-(으)면 안 되다). Source: textbook p. 18.
- **-(으)ㄹ 테니까** — The speaker offers a plan or intention as the reason for a request or suggestion. -을 테니까 follows a consonant; -ㄹ 테니까 follows a vowel or ㄹ. Full clauses are shown because the following request gives essential context. Source: textbook p. 19.
- **-(ㄴ/는)다 / -았/었다** — Diary and written plain statements: present action verbs take -ㄴ다 after a vowel or ㄹ (drop ㄹ), -는다 after other consonants. Present adjectives and 있다/없다 keep -다. Past verbs and adjectives take -았/었다. Source: textbook p. 28.
- **-(으)ㄴ/는/(으)ㄹ 것 같다** — Express an inference, not certainty. Past actions use -(으)ㄴ, current actions and 있다/없다 use -는, current adjectives use -(으)ㄴ, and predictions use -(으)ㄹ 것 같다. Nouns use 인 것 같다. Source: textbook p. 29.

### Unit 4: School life and worries

- **-(으)ㄹ까 봐** — Describe worry that an unwanted possibility may happen, or an action taken to prevent it. Use -을까 봐 after a consonant and -ㄹ까 봐 after a vowel or ㄹ. Verbs and adjectives both work. Source: textbook p. 48.
- **-느라고** — An action occupies the subject and causes a negative result or prevents another action. Attach -느라고 directly to action stems (drop ㄹ); do not add a past-tense marker. Normally both clauses share the subject. Not used with adjective stems. Source: textbook p. 49.

### Unit 5: Reporting school events

- **(이)래(요)** — Report someone else’s noun statement, shortened from -(이)라고 해요. After a consonant use 이래요, after a vowel 래요. This is reported information, not a direct identity statement or a command. Source: textbook p. 58.
- **-(ㄴ/는)대(요)** — Relay a declarative statement: present action verbs use -ㄴ대 after a vowel or ㄹ (drop ㄹ), -는대 after other consonants. Adjectives and 있다/없다 use -대. Past statements use -았/었대. Add 요 for polite speech. Source: textbook p. 59.

### Unit 7: Shopping and wishes

- **-아/어도** — Connect a verb or adjective with -아/어도 to say “even if/even though.” The second clause still holds despite the first. This is concession; -아/어도 되다 adds permission. 하다 becomes 해도. Source: textbook p. 78.
- **-(으)면 좋겠다** — Use -(으)면 좋겠다 to express a wish: “I hope…” or “It would be nice if….” Add -으면 after a consonant and -면 after a vowel or ㄹ; apply irregular stem changes. 좋겠어요 is polite; 좋겠어 is familiar. Source: textbook p. 79.

### Unit 8: Watching and giving impressions

- **-아/어 보이다** — Use an adjective with -아/어 보이다 to describe how someone or something looks or seems from observation, rather than stating a known fact. 하다 adjectives become -해 보이다; apply irregular changes (춥다 → 추워). 보여요 is polite; 보여 is familiar. Source: textbook p. 88.
- **-(으)면서** — Use -(으)면서 for two actions happening at the same time, usually by the same subject. Add -으면서 after a consonant and -면서 after a vowel or ㄹ. 듣다 becomes 들으면서. The final verb carries tense and politeness. Source: textbook p. 89.

### Unit 10: Symptoms and experiences

- **-는/(으)ㄴ데** — Use -는데 with present verbs and 있다/없다, and -(으)ㄴ데 with present adjectives, to give background for a following question, request or statement. Nouns take -인데; past forms take -았/었는데. This connective introduces context; it does not itself mark a reason or goal. Source: textbook p. 108.
- **-아/어 보다** — Use a verb with -아/어 보다 to describe trying an action. For an experience already had, use -아/어 봤어요 (familiar: 봤어); a negative experience can be 아직 안 … 봤어요. 하다 becomes 해 보다. This lesson focuses on experience, not an adjective’s appearance. Source: textbook p. 109.

### Unit 11: Healthy habits and advice

- **-(으)려면** — Use -(으)려면 to introduce a desired goal and the condition or action needed to reach it: “If you want to….” Add -으려면 after a consonant and -려면 after a vowel or ㄹ. Adjective examples describe a desired change or outcome, such as 키가 크려면. A following clause often gives advice or a requirement. Source: textbook p. 118.
- **-기 때문에** — Use a verb or adjective stem + -기 때문에 for a reason: “because….” Past reasons use -았/었기 때문에; nouns take -(이)기 때문에. The reason precedes the result. Keep tense accurate, and distinguish a reason from a desired goal. Source: textbook p. 119.

### Unit 13: Dreams and careers

- **-는지 알다/모르다** — Embed an information question before 알다/모르다: present action verbs use -는지. Past actions use -았/었는지; adjective stems use -(으)ㄴ지, and nouns use -인지. The question word remains inside the embedded clause. 있다/없다 are exceptions to ordinary adjective forms: use 있는지/없는지. Source: textbook p. 138.
- **-는 것** — Use present action verb + -는 것 to turn an action into a noun phrase, or to refer to the thing someone does. Add particles to 것: 것이 for the subject and 것을 for the object. Adjectives instead take -(으)ㄴ 것, and nouns take -인 것. Source: textbook p. 139.

### Unit 14: Camping and reported conversations

- **-재(요)** — Report a suggestion with action verb + -재(요), the contraction of -자고 해(요). It means someone suggests doing something together, rather than reporting a command or statement. Use -지 말재요 for a negative suggestion. Source: textbook p. 148.
- **-느냬(요)/(으)냬(요)** — Report a question: action verbs take -느냬(요); adjectives take -(으)냬(요), with -으냬 after a consonant and -냬 after a vowel. Past questions take -았/었냬(요). These contract reported questions with -냐고 해(요). For this course, 있다/없다 use -느냬(요), as in 시간이 있느냬요, rather than the ordinary adjective ending. Source: textbook p. 149.

## Audio and source scope

The original nineteen pilot cards retain their existing synthesized audio. New audio is generated entirely locally with installed Korean Yuna and English Samantha voices. It is a listening model, not a pronunciation assessment. Dictionary endings, casual quotations, and polite requests follow the source's register; explain who is speaking and to whom. Clause fragments have a continuation in their English cue and need a following request, suggestion or result.

Card words and grammar models are grounded in cited textbook/workbook pages. Quiz contexts are authored applications rather than quotations. Source transcripts are evidence aids; use the PDFs for illustrations and layout. The book's listening, conversation, longer reading, open writing, games, and cultural activities are not replaced by multiple choice. Do those together or with a teacher. The course includes source references so these activities remain part of the learner's work.
