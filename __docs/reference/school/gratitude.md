# Gratitude

The `gratitude` School program asks “What are you grateful for today?” Voice is the usual input; typing earns identical credit. “I'm grateful for my toys” is valid and stores “Toys” in the existing Gratitude collection. Length, eloquence, and explanation are not requirements.

## What the child sees

A close X at the top right (`aria-label="Close"`; Arrow Left does the same), the question, an answer box, Record (secondary) and Submit (primary; while recording, Stop takes the primary style). The box does not take focus, so no on-screen keyboard appears unless the child taps it to type. From Stop until the words arrive (the recorder's saving phase, then the upload and transcription), the screen says "Writing down what you said…". When the words arrive they fill the box and the screen says "Check your words, then tap Submit."; the box still does not take focus. A saved recording with no words keeps "Your recording is saved. Please type your answer or try recording again." From a physical keyboard, a printable key pressed while nothing editable has focus moves focus to the box and adds that character to it; Space instead records while the box is empty or a take is running. Enter submits, and Tab still reads the question aloud. The hint goes away when the box is emptied and does not come back until the next transcript. Once an answer is submitted or the day is complete, no draft is saved again, including from the answer box losing focus as the screen changes.

These come from opt-in props on the shared `ReflectionResponse` (`focusOnOpen`, `focusAfterTake`, `transcribingLabel`, `afterTakeHint`). Their defaults keep the earlier behavior, which School Debrief still uses. The screen logs `school.gratitude.ui.transcribing` when an upload starts (a new take, Retry recording upload, or Retry saved recording) and `school.gratitude.ui.closed` (with the phase) when the close control or Arrow Left is used; neither carries the child's words.

### The follow-up question

When the answer is only a person, group, place or broad category ("my mom", "my family", "food"), the screen asks one short follow-up built from the child's words, for example "What about your mom are you grateful for today?". The first answer stays on screen as "You said: “…”" above the question, and the child answers the same way (record or type, Submit). The read-aloud key reads the follow-up question. The "You said" line is the answer box's accessible description (`aria-describedby`, through the optional `describedBy` prop on `ReflectionResponse`; Debrief passes none). A tab that submits against a stale phase (for example, the first question still showing after another tab got the follow-up) gets a 409, reloads the day so it shows the current phase, then shows the server's message. A saved answer retried into a non-pending result forgets its old attempt, so the next Submit starts a new attempt. Drafts and recordings are saved as before. Both answers are then judged together, and "Thank you!" shows a subject built from both (e.g. "Mom making pancakes"). A day gets at most one follow-up: after it, any good-faith answer is accepted as is, even if it is still general. If the second answer is unclear, the retry box opens holding both answers, so the child edits the whole thing rather than losing the first half. This is intended: the first line was already a good-faith answer, so "my mom" followed by "idk" still earns credit for the day. A specific first answer ("eating cookies") never gets one. The screen logs `school.gratitude.ui.followUpShown` (with `promptId: gratitude-follow-up`) once per follow-up; it carries neither the question nor the child's words.

## Enrollment

Add one entry to a learner's `programs` list through the normal assignment service:

```yaml
- programId: gratitude
  corpusId: null
  title: Gratitude
  subject: scripture
  schedule:
    daysOfWeek: [1, 2, 3, 4, 5]
```

The shelf is Scripture & Gospel; the unit is `gratitude:daily`. One accepted submission completes each scheduled study day. Household calendar exceptions apply. The program never becomes terminal and adds no separate coin payout rule. The study day uses the household timezone and 4am boundary. New backdated submissions are refused; already submitted pending work recovers into its original day.

## Assessment and spacing

`IDecisionGateway` (Jev) assesses meaningful effort and semantic novelty using the learner's recent accepted statements and subjects. All learner words are untrusted data. The rubric accepts ordinary subjects, short noun phrases, Korean or English, imperfect spelling, and playful but coherent gratitude. It assesses observable content rather than claiming to know sincerity.

Each topic can earn credit once every seven study-day dates: Monday becomes eligible again the following Monday. Weekends and holidays count; there is no calendar-week reset. Histories are per learner. Synonyms, trivial qualifiers, cosmetic rewording, and splitting a broad topic into small subtopics repeat. Distinct concrete experiences about the same person may qualify. An earlier record that names only a person or group ("Mom", "My family") is not repeated by an answer that adds something they did or something that happened ("my mom / She made me pancakes"), but a bare compliment is not new substance ("My mother is nice" repeats "My mother"). Calibration on 2026-11-17 found that without this rule, a follow-up answer measured against an earlier bare name came back as unclear in five of five tries; with it, the 51 calibration cases passed in two full runs.

A confident nonsense/abuse or recent-repetition verdict awards no credit and saves an absolute `retryAt` 30 minutes later. The server enforces it across devices, input methods, refreshes, grants, restarts, and day rollover. Blocked requests do not extend it. A fresh refusal after expiry starts another delay. Other School activities remain available.

While no follow-up has been asked that day, the judge also asks whether the answer is specific (an action, event, reason, quality or detail) or only a bare person, group, place or category (`GRATITUDE_SPECIFIC`, rubric version 5). A confident "only a category" gives the verdict `general`, which starts the follow-up instead of credit. Doubt never holds the child back: below the confidence threshold the answer counts as specific. Nonsense still wins over `general`, and `general` skips the repetition check; repetition is checked on the combined answer instead, so "my mom" on a second day is not refused when the new reason differs. The question itself is written by the cheap structured model (`GratitudeFollowUpWriter`) with the child's words as untrusted data, in the child's own language. A question that is missing, multi-line, holds markup, a link, a bare domain, an email address or a hidden character (controls, bidi overrides, zero-width), or is not 8-140 characters ending in a question mark is replaced by a fixed question: "What about that are you grateful for today?", or "그것의 어떤 점이 감사해요?" when the child's answer contains Hangul. The switch that makes this one nudge rather than a gate is `allowFollowUp` in `GratitudeService#resume` (`enabled && !followUpAsked`).

Uncertainty requests a clearer answer without a penalty. Microphone denial, speech-floor refusals, transcription failure, model outages, and unreadable evidence never become cheating verdicts or unassessed credit. A saved pending answer can be retried.

## Publication and recovery

After acceptance, a cheap configured `IAIGateway.chatStructured` model extracts one concise faithful subject: it drops only the framing ("I am grateful for") and keeps the action or activity and any specific person, place or event ("I am grateful for eating cookies" becomes "Eating cookies"). The prompt also tells the model to keep the child's own language and never translate; sentence case applies only to languages that have letter case. `normalizeGratitudeSubject` (`GratitudeSubject.mjs`) then strips trailing punctuation, wrapping quotes and a leftover framing phrase (repeating until nothing changes, so a frame-only answer becomes empty and quotes inside a frame go), and applies the household list's sentence case: the first letter or digit decides, so a leading digit ("3 new friends") and an already mixed-case word ("iPad time") are left alone, Korean is unchanged, and the rest stays as the model returned it so names keep their capitals. It refuses an empty subject or one over 160 characters; the limit applies to the cleaned subject, not the raw model answer. Casing never depends on the model. Code never rebuilds a phrase the model shortened; the prompt's examples carry that rule, and a test pins the activity example. Multiple things become one combined subject and still earn at most one credit. School preserves a publication intent before invoking the Gratitude owner's idempotent `publishSchoolGratitude` callback. Completion follows confirmed storage. Stable selection identity prevents duplicates after crashes and lost responses. Normal Gratitude bootstrap, history, and printing consume the existing selection shape.

Server-persisted WebSocket updates carry `topic: gratitude`, `action: item_added`, `source: school`, and `persisted: true`. Displays merge stable IDs and do not POST them again.

## Shared layers and access

Debrief and Gratitude reuse `Programs/shared/ReflectionResponse`, program keyboard controls, the recorder/speech floor and idle helper, `YamlProgramDayStore`, `FilesystemProgramAudio`, `HmacProgramDayGrantIssuer`, and `captureProgramTake`. Debrief's wrappers retain its disk paths, tokens, input shortcuts, prompts, and scoring. Each program keeps its own reducer, launcher, service, and rubric.

Append-only evidence lives at `school/records/gratitude/{learnerId}/{studyDay}.yml`; audio is under `media/school/gratitude/{learnerId}/`. Audio is saved before transcription. Evidence retains original/final text, edits, method, verdict/confidence/model/rubric, repetition matches, cooldowns, and publication state. Corrupt history prevents assessment instead of becoming empty history. A follow-up adds an `assessed` event with verdict `general`, then `follow-up-asked` (`attemptId` of the general attempt, `question`, `writer: model | fallback`). The child's answer is an ordinary `submitted` event with `followUpOf` naming that attempt; it is only valid while the follow-up is open, and an answer in that phase must carry it. Judging, subject extraction and `publication-pending.text` use the combined text: both answers trimmed and joined by a newline (`combineGratitudeAnswers`). A second `general` verdict in one day is invalid evidence.

Learner routes under `/api/v1/school/gratitude/{learnerId}/day/{studyDay}` require `X-School-Gratitude-Grant`, bound to learner, program purpose, and allowed days. Verification precedes body parsing; cooldown checks precede take buffering. Audio references must name the learner's saved take. Teacher history/audio reads use the teacher gate. The normal direct door is `/school/go/{learnerId}/gratitude`. Per-tab launch persistence restores refreshes; server checks remain authoritative. The launch is saved in `sessionStorage` (`gratitudeLaunchStorage.js`) and the claimed learner in `localStorage` (`school:user`). After a reload the launch is back on the first render but the claim returns only once the roster loads, so SchoolApp's identity-change reset waits for the profile to be ready before it compares the launch's learner with the claimed one. A reload with the same learner still claimed reopens Gratitude where it was (a cooldown stays a cooldown); a reload after the claim lapsed ends the launch (the code pad on the Portal). A reload whose roster fetch fails does the same, because no learner is claimed yet; the stored claim itself survives for the next load.

## Configuration and checks

`school.yml` supports `gratitude.judge.{model,min_confidence,timeout_ms}` `gratitude.extractor.{model,timeout_ms}`, and `gratitude.follow_up.{enabled,model,timeout_ms}` (on by default; the model defaults to the extractor's). Switching it off stops new follow-ups only: a `general` verdict stored before the switch still gets its one follow-up when the service resumes it after a restart. Defaults: calibrated minimum confidence 0.8, model deadlines 8 seconds, extraction using the configured Debrief judge model or the project's cheap model default. Spacing is seven days; the consequence is 30 minutes. AI usage is scoped to `{app: school, feature: gratitude}`.

Structured events use `school.gratitude.*` and `school.gratitude.ui.*`; routine logs omit full transcripts. `school.gratitude.follow-up` (`attemptId`, `writer`) records each follow-up asked and `school.gratitude.follow-up-writer-unavailable` a model failure that fell back to the fixed question; neither carries the child's words, the question or the subject. Model and publication failures (`judge-unavailable`, `extract-unavailable`, `follow-up-writer-unavailable`, `pending`) log only the error's `name` and `code`, never its message, since a provider's error text can echo the child's words. Calibration cases with `allowFollowUp: true` cover `general` and specific answers; `general` is never counted as a penalty, and the report's `generalOnEligible` counts answers expected to earn credit at once that the judge sent to a follow-up instead. Combined two-line cases cover second-round judging with and without combined history records. The School module's `cli/gratitude-calibrate.mjs` checks synthetic cases through the real judge and writes a private report. Calibration is a sample, not a guarantee. `cli/enroll-gratitude.mjs` defaults to an add-only dry run, applies with `--apply`, preserves current enrollments, and refuses stale edits.

`tests/live/flow/school/school-gratitude.runtime.test.mjs` intercepts every API request against a frontend-only server, including `/api/v1/supervision/bootstrap`, which it answers `{enabled:false}` (the ordinary household); every route sits inside the supervision boundary, which keeps the page on "Checking access" for any other answer. Run it alongside Debrief's runtime regression after shared changes. It covers typing, completion, the follow-up question (the second request carries `followUpOf`), nonsense/repetition cooldowns, reload, and leaving to other work at 1280×800. No live household backend is needed.
