# Gratitude

The `gratitude` School program asks “What are you grateful for today?” Voice is the usual input; typing earns identical credit. “I'm grateful for my toys” is valid and stores “Toys” in the existing Gratitude collection. Length, eloquence, and explanation are not requirements.

## What the child sees

The header names the child (avatar and name) and has a close X at the top right (`aria-label="Close"`; Arrow Left does the same). Below it: the question, an answer box, Record (secondary) and Submit (primary; while recording, Stop takes the primary style). The box does not take focus, so no on-screen keyboard appears unless the child taps it to type. From Stop until the words arrive (the recorder's saving phase, then the upload and transcription), the screen says "Writing down what you said…". When the words arrive they replace whatever was in the box (a take is a whole answer, never added to the last one; `replaceOnTake` on `ReflectionResponse`) and the screen says "Check your words, then tap Submit."; the box still does not take focus. A take with no words of the child's (an empty transcript, or the transcription prompt echoed back on silence, whole or in pieces such as "in English or Korean", with at most one other word; `isUnheardTranscript`) leaves the box as it was and says "I didn't hear you. Try again."  A saved recording with no words keeps "Your recording is saved. Please type your answer or try recording again." From a physical keyboard, a printable key pressed while nothing editable has focus moves focus to the box and adds that character to it; Space instead records while the box is empty or a take is running. Enter submits, and Tab still reads the question aloud. The hint goes away when the box is emptied and does not come back until the next transcript. Once an answer is submitted or the day is complete, no draft is saved again, including from the answer box losing focus as the screen changes.

These come from opt-in props on the shared `ReflectionResponse` (`focusOnOpen`, `focusAfterTake`, `transcribingLabel`, `afterTakeHint`, `replaceOnTake`). Their defaults keep the earlier behavior, which School Debrief still uses. The screen logs `school.gratitude.ui.transcribing` when an upload starts (a new take, Retry recording upload, or Retry saved recording) and `school.gratitude.ui.closed` (with the phase) when the close control or Arrow Left is used; neither carries the child's words.

### The follow-up question

When the answer is only a person, group, place or broad category ("my mom", "my family", "food"), the screen asks one short follow-up built from the child's words, for example "What about your mom are you grateful for today?". The first answer stays on screen as "You said: “…”" above the question, and the child answers the same way (record or type, Submit). The read-aloud key reads the follow-up question. The "You said" line is the answer box's accessible description (`aria-describedby`, through the optional `describedBy` prop on `ReflectionResponse`; Debrief passes none). A tab that submits against a stale phase (for example, the first question still showing after another tab got the follow-up) gets a 409, reloads the day so it shows the current phase, then shows the server's message. A saved answer retried into a non-pending result forgets its old attempt, so the next Submit starts a new attempt. Drafts and recordings are saved as before. Both answers are then judged together, and "Thank you!" shows a subject built from both (e.g. "Mom making pancakes"). A day gets at most one follow-up: after it, any good-faith answer is accepted as is, even if it is still general. If the second answer is unclear, the retry box opens empty, like every retry. This is intended: the first line was already a good-faith answer, so "my mom" followed by "idk" still earns credit for the day. A specific first answer ("eating cookies") never gets one. The screen logs `school.gratitude.ui.followUpShown` (with `promptId: gratitude-follow-up`) once per follow-up; it carries neither the question nor the child's words.

## Preschool mode

Learners listed in `school.yml → gratitude.preschool` (a list of learner ids) get full-screen picture-and-voice screens and a gentler verdict ladder. Everyone else keeps the text-box screen above. The view carries `mode: preschool | standard`; the server decides it.

| Verdict | Screen | Spoken line | Counts toward a break |
|---|---|---|---|
| eligible | Green, check icon, the subject with a picture, a chime | "Thank you! I'll remember: cookies." | no |
| uncertain (judge cannot decide) | Green; the `assessed` event is `verdict: eligible`, `judged: uncertain`, `flagged: uncertain` | same as green | no |
| repetition / general | Blue "Try something new": the used subject greyed as "already used", picture hints for categories not used recently | "You said cookies on Tuesday. What's something different? What about Mom?" | no |
| nonsense, first in a run | Yellow "That was silly" | "That sounded silly. Tell me one real thing you liked today." | yes |
| nonsense, second in a run, or rude | Red break: a ring empties over 5 minutes, then a fresh Ask | "Let's take a break and try again soon." | — |
| nothing heard (empty, or the prompt echoed back) | Ask again: "I didn't hear you. Try again." | — | not judged |

- After two blue verdicts in a row, the next good-faith answer is accepted and flagged (`flagged: blue-limit`; `judged:` the original verdict when it was not eligible). A yellow in between breaks the row.
- A day that already had its follow-up (asked before the learner moved to preschool mode) gets no specificity question; a bare answer is accepted and flagged `general`. A verdict the evidence chain would refuse is never appended: it falls back to green+flag (`uncertain`) for preschool, an unclear retry otherwise, and logs `school.gratitude.verdict-invalid`. A submit that finds an attempt left assessing retries that attempt first.
- The silly count resets after a green or after a finished break. A blue does not reset it.
- The judge asks one more yes/no question for these learners, `GRATITUDE_RUDE` (insults, name-calling or swearing; silliness and grumbling are not rude). A confident yes is the verdict `rude`, which goes straight to the red break. The `specific` question still runs, so a bare category is steered blue instead of getting the follow-up; preschool mode never asks a follow-up. Rubric version 6.
- The break is 5 minutes (`PRESCHOOL_COOLDOWN_MS`); the `refused` event records `cooldownMs: 300000`. Other learners' 30 minutes are unchanged and their events carry no `cooldownMs`.
- Only green earns credit. Nothing takes credit away. A flag is a mark for the teacher in the history (`GET /teacher/{learnerId}/history`), not a penalty.

Screens, one big action each, with the child's avatar and name large in the header on every one:

1. **Ask.** "What made you happy today?" is shown and spoken on open. Four picture hints (Family, Food, Toys, Outside); tapping one speaks an example ("What about Mom?"). One giant mic. No text box, and typing does nothing.
2. **Listening.** The mic glows with the voice level. It stops on its own after the child goes quiet (the shared speech floor's auto-stop) or on a tap.
3. **"Did you say…?"** The transcript big and spoken. Yes submits it; Say it again throws the take away and records a fresh one. Takes never pile up.
4. **Checking.** A spinner while the take is written down or the answer is judged; nothing can be tapped.
5. **Result.** Green, blue, yellow or red, per the table. Blue and yellow carry the mic for the next try. Red shows the time left as an emptying ring and has no action; being idle through it does not close the program, and when it ends the Ask comes back.

Re-entering during a break shows the ring with the time left; re-entering after green shows the green screen. A follow-up left open from before the switch is asked as a blue screen and answered as a follow-up. A failed request shows "Something went wrong" with one "Tap to try again" button, which reopens the day. The keyboard does nothing on these screens, and Close is locked while checking. Speech is cancelled before every recording and when the screen closes, so a take never records the screen's own voice. A learner taken out of preschool mode with a `steered` or `warned` day sees the text box as a retry. Icons are generated Solar icons (`check`, `idea`, `warning`, `hourglass`, `microphone`, `heart`, `family`, `food`, `toys`, `outside`); there are no face icons. Colours are the design-system status roles (`--ds-success`, `--ds-info`, `--ds-warning`, `--ds-danger`).

To put a learner in preschool mode:

```yaml
# household school.yml
gratitude:
  preschool: [kid]
```

## Enrollment

Add one entry to a learner's `programs` list through the normal assignment service:

```yaml
- programId: gratitude
  corpusId: null
  title: Gratitude
  subject: scripture
  schedule:
    daysOfWeek: [1, 2, 3, 4, 5]
```

`daysOfWeek` is ISO (1 = Monday … 7 = Sunday). For a daily habit that runs on weekends too, list all seven: `[1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7]`.

The shelf is Scripture & Gospel; the unit is `gratitude:daily`. One accepted submission completes each scheduled study day. Household calendar exceptions apply. The program never becomes terminal and adds no separate coin payout rule. The study day uses the household timezone and 4am boundary. New backdated submissions are refused; already submitted pending work recovers into its original day.

## Assessment and spacing

`IDecisionGateway` (Jev) assesses meaningful effort and semantic novelty using the learner's recent accepted statements and subjects. All learner words are untrusted data. The rubric accepts ordinary subjects, short noun phrases, Korean or English, imperfect spelling, and playful but coherent gratitude. It assesses observable content rather than claiming to know sincerity.

Each topic can earn credit once every seven study-day dates: Monday becomes eligible again the following Monday. Weekends and holidays count; there is no calendar-week reset. Histories are per learner. Synonyms, trivial qualifiers, cosmetic rewording, and splitting a broad topic into small subtopics repeat. Distinct concrete experiences about the same person may qualify. An earlier record that names only a person or group ("Mom", "My family") is not repeated by an answer that adds something they did or something that happened ("my mom / She made me pancakes"), but a bare compliment is not new substance ("My mother is nice" repeats "My mother"). Calibration on 2026-11-17 found that without this rule, a follow-up answer measured against an earlier bare name came back as unclear in five of five tries; with it, the 51 calibration cases passed in two full runs.

For learners not in preschool mode, a confident nonsense/abuse or recent-repetition verdict awards no credit and saves an absolute `retryAt` 30 minutes later (preschool mode has its own ladder, below). The server enforces it across devices, input methods, refreshes, grants, restarts, and day rollover. Blocked requests do not extend it. A fresh refusal after expiry starts another delay. Other School activities remain available.

While no follow-up has been asked that day, the judge also asks whether the answer is specific (an action, event, reason, quality or detail) or only a bare person, group, place or category (`GRATITUDE_SPECIFIC`, rubric version 5). A confident "only a category" gives the verdict `general`, which starts the follow-up instead of credit. Doubt never holds the child back: below the confidence threshold the answer counts as specific. Nonsense still wins over `general`, and `general` skips the repetition check; repetition is checked on the combined answer instead, so "my mom" on a second day is not refused when the new reason differs. The question itself is written by the cheap structured model (`GratitudeFollowUpWriter`) with the child's words as untrusted data, in the child's own language. A question that is missing, multi-line, holds markup, a link, a bare domain, an email address or a hidden character (controls, bidi overrides, zero-width), or is not 8-140 characters ending in a question mark is replaced by a fixed question: "What about that are you grateful for today?", or "그것의 어떤 점이 감사해요?" when the child's answer contains Hangul. The switch that makes this one nudge rather than a gate is `allowFollowUp` in `GratitudeService#resume` (`enabled && !followUpAsked`).

Uncertainty requests a clearer answer without a penalty, and the retry box opens empty: a refused or unclear answer never stays in the box. A submit that arrives while another attempt for the learner is still being judged (repeated taps, a second tab) gets the current state back and starts no attempt and no judge call; the screen's Submit is locked meanwhile. Microphone denial, speech-floor refusals, transcription failure, model outages, and unreadable evidence never become cheating verdicts or unassessed credit. A saved pending answer can be retried.

## Publication and recovery

After acceptance, a cheap configured `IAIGateway.chatStructured` model extracts one concise faithful subject: it drops only the framing ("I am grateful for") and keeps the action or activity and any specific person, place or event ("I am grateful for eating cookies" becomes "Eating cookies"). The prompt also tells the model to keep the child's own language and never translate; sentence case applies only to languages that have letter case. `normalizeGratitudeSubject` (`GratitudeSubject.mjs`) then strips trailing punctuation, wrapping quotes and a leftover framing phrase (repeating until nothing changes, so a frame-only answer becomes empty and quotes inside a frame go), and applies the household list's sentence case: the first letter or digit decides, so a leading digit ("3 new friends") and an already mixed-case word ("iPad time") are left alone, Korean is unchanged, and the rest stays as the model returned it so names keep their capitals. It refuses an empty subject or one over 160 characters; the limit applies to the cleaned subject, not the raw model answer. Casing never depends on the model. Code never rebuilds a phrase the model shortened; the prompt's examples carry that rule, and a test pins the activity example. Multiple things become one combined subject and still earn at most one credit. School preserves a publication intent before invoking the Gratitude owner's idempotent `publishSchoolGratitude` callback. Completion follows confirmed storage. Stable selection identity prevents duplicates after crashes and lost responses. Normal Gratitude bootstrap, history, and printing consume the existing selection shape.

Server-persisted WebSocket updates carry `topic: gratitude`, `action: item_added`, `source: school`, and `persisted: true`. Displays merge stable IDs and do not POST them again.

## Shared layers and access

Debrief and Gratitude reuse `Programs/shared/ReflectionResponse`, program keyboard controls, the recorder/speech floor and idle helper, `YamlProgramDayStore`, `FilesystemProgramAudio`, `HmacProgramDayGrantIssuer`, and `captureProgramTake`. Debrief's wrappers retain its disk paths, tokens, input shortcuts, prompts, and scoring. Each program keeps its own reducer, launcher, service, and rubric.

Append-only evidence lives at `school/records/gratitude/{learnerId}/{studyDay}.yml`; audio is under `media/school/gratitude/{learnerId}/`. Audio is saved before transcription. Evidence retains original/final text, edits, method, verdict/confidence/model/rubric, repetition matches, cooldowns, and publication state. Corrupt history prevents assessment instead of becoming empty history. A follow-up adds an `assessed` event with verdict `general`, then `follow-up-asked` (`attemptId` of the general attempt, `question`, `writer: model | fallback`). The child's answer is an ordinary `submitted` event with `followUpOf` naming that attempt; it is only valid while the follow-up is open, and an answer in that phase must carry it. Judging, subject extraction and `publication-pending.text` use the combined text: both answers trimmed and joined by a newline (`combineGratitudeAnswers`). A second `general` verdict in one day is invalid evidence. A new take appends `take-saved` (it replaces the draft); a take with nothing heard appends `take-unheard` (`takeId`, `audioRef`), which puts back the draft from before that take. Preschool mode adds `steered` (`reason: general | repetition`, `matchedId` for a repeat), `warned`, the `assessed` fields `flagged` and `judged`, the verdict `rude`, and `refused.cooldownMs`; a `refused` event's `retryAt` must equal the assessment time plus its `cooldownMs` (30 minutes when absent, never more).

Learner routes under `/api/v1/school/gratitude/{learnerId}/day/{studyDay}` require `X-School-Gratitude-Grant`, bound to learner, program purpose, and allowed days. Verification precedes body parsing; cooldown checks precede take buffering. Audio references must name the learner's saved take. Teacher history/audio reads use the teacher gate. The normal direct door is `/school/go/{learnerId}/gratitude`. A self-service code for Gratitude opens it directly, re-entered or not: a card whose only action is Gratitude, the Debrief or the reading shelf never asks "Is this you?" (see the School README); other programs still ask. Per-tab launch persistence restores refreshes; server checks remain authoritative. The launch is saved in `sessionStorage` (`gratitudeLaunchStorage.js`) and the claimed learner in `localStorage` (`school:user`). After a reload the launch is back on the first render but the claim returns only once the roster loads, so SchoolApp's identity-change reset waits for the profile to be ready before it compares the launch's learner with the claimed one. A reload with the same learner still claimed reopens Gratitude where it was (a cooldown stays a cooldown); a reload after the claim lapsed ends the launch (the code pad on the Portal). A reload whose roster fetch fails does the same, because no learner is claimed yet; the stored claim itself survives for the next load.

## Configuration and checks

`school.yml` supports `gratitude.judge.{model,min_confidence,timeout_ms}` `gratitude.extractor.{model,timeout_ms}`, and `gratitude.follow_up.{enabled,model,timeout_ms}` (on by default; the model defaults to the extractor's). Switching it off stops new follow-ups only: a `general` verdict stored before the switch still gets its one follow-up when the service resumes it after a restart. Defaults: calibrated minimum confidence 0.8, model deadlines 8 seconds, extraction using the configured Debrief judge model or the project's cheap model default. Spacing is seven days; the consequence is 30 minutes (5 for learners in `gratitude.preschool`, see Preschool mode). AI usage is scoped to `{app: school, feature: gratitude}`.

Structured events use `school.gratitude.*` and `school.gratitude.ui.*`; routine logs omit full transcripts. `school.gratitude.take-unheard` (`takeId`, `empty`) records a take with nothing heard. Preschool mode adds `school.gratitude.steered` (`reason`), `.warned`, `.flagged` (`reason: uncertain | blue-limit`), `.refused` with `reason: rude`, and `school.gratitude.ui.screen` (`screen: ask | listening | confirm | checking | green | blue | yellow | red`) on every screen change. `school.gratitude.follow-up` (`attemptId`, `writer`) records each follow-up asked and `school.gratitude.follow-up-writer-unavailable` a model failure that fell back to the fixed question; neither carries the child's words, the question or the subject. Model and publication failures (`judge-unavailable`, `extract-unavailable`, `follow-up-writer-unavailable`, `pending`) log only the error's `name` and `code`, never its message, since a provider's error text can echo the child's words. Calibration cases with `allowFollowUp: true` cover `general` and specific answers; `general` is never counted as a penalty, and the report's `generalOnEligible` counts answers expected to earn credit at once that the judge sent to a follow-up instead. Combined two-line cases cover second-round judging with and without combined history records. The School module's `cli/gratitude-calibrate.mjs` checks synthetic cases through the real judge and writes a private report. Calibration is a sample, not a guarantee. `cli/enroll-gratitude.mjs` defaults to an add-only dry run, applies with `--apply`, preserves current enrollments, and refuses stale edits.

`tests/live/flow/school/school-gratitude.runtime.test.mjs` intercepts every API request against a frontend-only server, including `/api/v1/supervision/bootstrap`, which it answers `{enabled:false}` (the ordinary household); every route sits inside the supervision boundary, which keeps the page on "Checking access" for any other answer. Run it alongside Debrief's runtime regression after shared changes. It covers typing, completion, the follow-up question (the second request carries `followUpOf`), nonsense/repetition cooldowns, reload, and leaving to other work at 1280×800. No live household backend is needed.
